# 4. The copper foil

The foil is not plain copper sheet. It is made for bonding.

## How it is made

- Copper is plated onto a turning drum and peeled off as a continuous foil.
  This is electrodeposited foil, the most common kind.
- The side that grew away from the drum is rough. It is called the **matte**
  side, and it is the side that bonds to the resin.

## The bonding treatment

Foil makers treat the matte side in steps:

1. A deposit of tiny copper nodules, to give the resin more surface to grip.
2. A thin zinc layer, as a barrier between the copper and the epoxy.
3. A chromium layer against tarnish.
4. A silane coupling agent.

One patent gives a typical peel strength of 10 to 14 pounds per inch for
35 µm treated foil on glass and epoxy.

## What it means for the trail

- Buy treated circuit board foil, and put the matte side against the resin.
- Plain copper sheet or craft foil has no treatment, and nothing found says
  how well it bonds.
