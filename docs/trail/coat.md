# 3. Coat and part-cure

This step exists only for the solvent routes. A wet lay-up skips it: the
resin goes on and the stack goes straight into the press.

## What the bake does

The coated cloth goes through an oven. The bake does two things at once:

1. It drives the solvent off.
2. It part-cures the resin, to what makers call the **B-stage**. The sheet is
   then dry to handle and still melts in the press.

The result is **prepreg**.

## Published bakes

All from the worked examples of company patents.

| Bake | Source |
|---|---|
| 155 °C for 7 minutes | US patent 9,139,750 |
| 163 °C for about 2.5 minutes | US patent 4,816,531 |
| 150 °C for 5 to 10 minutes, in hot air | US patent 5,726,257 |

## How much resin

A finished board is typically about half resin and half glass by weight.
The thin, open cloths carry more resin and the heavy ones less.

Resin content is the number to control. It sets thickness and dielectric
constant together. See [Resin content](../model/laminate.md).
